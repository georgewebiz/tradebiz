Core Principle / Taxonomy

Deconstructing Strategy Names & Layers

Different trading terms describe completely different layers of a chart. Separating these layers prevents false pattern recognition.

📍 Location Strategies

Describe where price is interacting on the chart, such as VWAP, the 9 EMA, the High of Day (HOD), premarket highs, or previous resistance levels. Locations identify areas to watch, but they are never automatic entry signals on their own.

📐 Shape Patterns

Describe the visual geometry of price action, such as bull flags, bear flags, pennants, or flat-top continuation formations. Shapes give visual structure but must be backed by market context and order-flow mechanics.

⚙️ Trading Mechanisms

Describe active buyer and seller behavior—how supply and demand interact to produce breakouts, expansions, retests, contractions, or reclaims.

02 / Recognize the mechanism

The Six Fundamental Momentum Trades

Filter by trade type. Review what constitutes a strong execution versus its specific failure clue.

↗

1. The Breakout

Can price escape a defined range?

Price escapes a defined range (e.g., trading between $49 and $50 where sellers repeatedly appeared). When buying pressure overwhelms sellers, price moves through $50 and expands higher. Note: The same breakout pattern means very different things depending on whether it occurs at the start of a higher-time-frame trend or at the exhausted end of an extended move.

Strong breakout criteriaMeaningful level, constructive approach, expanding volume participation, ample room to next major resistance, and rapid tape confirmation (failed selling attempts).
Failure clueQuick bull trap rejection back inside the range.
↗↘↗

2. Breakout & Retest

Resistance becomes support

A stock breaks above $50 to $50.70 and pulls back toward $50.10. The primary opportunity is testing whether buyers defend the former resistance level as new support. Defining feature: The market's specific response to the test of the broken boundary.

Look forPrice returns to the broken level, stabilizes, and buyers step in to resume upward movement.
Failure clueLevel fails to hold; breakdown through support with failed reclaim attempts.
⌁↗

3. Pullback Continuation

Impulse → retrace → resume

An established move (e.g., $100 to $104) retraces slightly to $103 or $102.75. Selling slows and buyers return. The trader bets that prevailing buying pressure remains intact and the countertrend move has not damaged the prevailing trend structure. Encompasses first/second pullbacks, 9 EMA pullbacks, VWAP pullbacks, and micro pullbacks.

Look forControlled retracement, fading selling pressure, and a crisp resumption trigger.
Failure clueSelling breaks structural swing lows or destroys trend integrity.
▱↗

4. Consolidation Continuation

Time compression → expansion

A pullback gives back price; a consolidation gives back time. A stock rising from $20 to $23 holds near $22.80–$22.90 in a narrow sideways range. Sellers cannot push price lower, and buyers prepare for a new leg. Appears as bull flags, pennants, flat-top continuations, or HOD consolidations.

Trigger & RiskRisk is placed right below the consolidation low; trigger is the upside break of the range.
Failure clueRepeated failed upside pushes or range breakdown.
⌁

5. Trend Continuation

Persistent directional structure

Focuses on persistent directional control rather than a single textbook pattern. In an uptrend, price repeatedly forms higher highs and higher lows, respecting trending channels or a rising 9 EMA. Warning: Do not use as a catch-all label for every moving chart; classify specific pullbacks or consolidations when present.

Look forUninterrupted structural momentum, shallow dips, and steady order flow.
Failure clueExhaustion spikes or structural trend breakage.
↘↗

6. The Reclaim

Lose level → trap → recovery

Price falls below an important level (like VWAP or premarket high) and remains below it, giving sellers control. Then price recovers the level and holds above it. Why it works: Short sellers who sold below the level are trapped and forced to cover, fueling rapid upside momentum.

Look forFast recovery, acceptance, and holding above the reclaimed level.
Failure clueImmediate re-loss of the reclaimed level.
Case Study / Practical Principle

The Same Location Means Different Things

Suppose VWAP is located exactly at $5.00. The location alone is never the trade—the price sequence dictates the mechanism.

All three scenarios involve the exact same price level ($5.00 VWAP), yet they represent completely different price action sequences and trade hypotheses:

Scenario A: Pullback ContinuationThe stock is trending strongly higher. It pulls back toward $5.00 (VWAP), selling slows, and price turns upward. The primary thesis is that existing upward momentum is resuming.
Scenario B: Breakout & RetestThe stock traded below $5.00, broke above it to $5.20, and returns to test $5.00 where buyers step in. The primary thesis is role reversal from resistance into support.
Scenario C: The ReclaimThe stock was above $5.00, lost VWAP, traded below it for a period, and then recovered $5.00, holding above it. The primary thesis is that the move below VWAP failed and trapped shorts.

The ASET Risk Model

Derived from SMB Capital's risk management architecture:

A — Allocation (Size)Position size & share count scaled strictly to your maximum allowable dollar risk limit.
S — Stop (Invalidation)Exact structural stop loss where trade thesis is proven wrong. Never arbitrary.
E — EntryPrecise execution price confirmed by your trigger at the opportunity zone.
T — TargetNext major resistance level, measured move objective, or trailing exit plan.

The Four Core Trade Management Decisions

Adapted from SMB Capital's PlayBook methodology:

1. Initial RiskDefine structural invalidation and calculate exact share size matching your risk limit.
2. First ObjectiveIdentify nearby resistance or measured move for potential partial profit-taking.
3. Continuation RuleDetermine specific tape evidence required to hold winning positions or scale in.
4. Exit & Stall RuleRecognize when momentum fades or tape slows, exiting before stop-outs occur.
